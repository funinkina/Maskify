\chapter{Introduction}
\section{Problem Statement}

The digital transformation of modern society has resulted in an exponential increase in the volume of documents containing sensitive personal information. Every day, organizations across healthcare, finance, legal, government, and educational sectors generate and process millions of documents containing personally identifiable information (PII) such as Aadhaar numbers, PAN cards, credit card details, addresses, and phone numbers. This widespread presence of sensitive data has created significant privacy and security challenges that traditional manual redaction methods struggle to address effectively.

The problem of sensitive information exposure in documents manifests in multiple critical ways. First, manual redaction is inherently time-consuming and labor-intensive. Processing a single document manually requires personnel to carefully examine each page, identify sensitive information, and manually black out or mask the identified data. When organizations process thousands of documents daily, this approach becomes completely unscalable. Second, human error introduces significant risk even when personnel attempt manual redaction. Studies have shown that manual redaction achieves accuracy rates between 85-95\% under optimal conditions, meaning that 5-15\% of sensitive information remains exposed or critical information is improperly removed. Third, the legal and regulatory landscape has become increasingly stringent, with penalties for data exposure reaching into billions of dollars. The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) has imposed fines exceeding 5.88 billion euros since its implementation, including a landmark 1.2 billion euro penalty against Meta in 2023. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 establishes comprehensive requirements for handling personal data, with specific provisions for penalty imposition for non-compliance.

The specific challenge this project addresses is the automated detection and redaction of Indian identity documents from digital files. India operates one of the world's largest identity systems through the Aadhaar program, which has enrolled over 1.3 billion residents. The Permanent Account Number (PAN) system managed by the Income Tax Department serves as another critical identifier for financial transactions. Payment card usage continues to grow exponentially with the digital payments ecosystem. The combination of widespread Aadhaar and PAN card usage in everyday transactions, combined with the increasing digitization of records across government and private sectors, creates a substantial attack surface for identity theft and fraud when documents are improperly handled or disposed of.

Existing redaction solutions in the market present several limitations. Commercial products like Adobe Acrobat offer basic redaction capabilities but lack specialized detection for Indian identity documents and do not implement mathematical checksum validation for detected numbers. Enterprise solutions often require expensive licensing and cloud processing, raising data privacy concerns as sensitive documents must be transmitted to external servers. Open-source alternatives like Microsoft Presidio provide pattern-based detection but primarily target Western document formats and lack the specific detection logic for Indian PII types. Most significantly, no existing solution combines the three critical features this project implements: mathematical checksum validation for detection accuracy, multi-orientation document handling for robust OCR, and tamper-evident audit logging for compliance verification.

\section{Motivation}

The motivation for this project stems from a confluence of practical requirements, technological opportunities, and the specific context of the Indian digital ecosystem.

From a practical standpoint, the handling of identity documents in Indian organizations presents unique challenges. Government offices, financial institutions, healthcare providers, educational institutions, and private businesses regularly process documents containing Aadhaar numbers, PAN card details, and payment information. The lack of specialized automated tools forces these organizations to rely on manual processes that are neither scalable nor reliable. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the need for vaccination certificates and identity verification dramatically increased document processing volumes, exposing the inadequacy of existing manual redaction capabilities. Similarly, the implementation of direct benefit transfer schemes, where government subsidies are transferred directly to Aadhaar-linked bank accounts, necessitates careful handling of identity documents throughout the process chain.

The technological opportunity arises from significant advances in optical character recognition and checksum validation algorithms that can be combined into a robust detection pipeline. Tesseract, the open-source OCR engine, has achieved maturity with support for LSTM-based neural network recognition, achieving accuracy rates sufficient for document processing. The mathematical foundations of error-detecting codes, specifically the Verhoeff algorithm for Aadhaar validation and the Luhn algorithm for payment card validation, provide deterministic verification that eliminates false positives from random number sequences. By combining these established algorithms with modern web technologies, the project creates a solution that is simultaneously more accurate and more accessible than existing commercial offerings.

The open-source nature of the solution addresses a critical gap in the current market. Organizations requiring PII redaction capabilities often face a difficult choice between expensive commercial products with uncertain data handling practices and basic open-source tools lacking enterprise features. This project demonstrates that it is possible to build a comprehensive solution using freely available technologies without compromising on features or accuracy. The zero-data-retention architecture ensures that sensitive documents never persist beyond the processing operation, addressing the primary concern organizations have about adopting cloud-based or third-party redaction services.

Furthermore, the project addresses an often-overlooked aspect of document processing: orientation handling. Real-world documents arrive in various orientations due to scanning artifacts, mobile photography, or physical document placement during digitization. Standard OCR systems assume correct orientation and fail when documents are rotated, leading to missed detections and incomplete redaction. The multi-orientation scanning approach implemented in this project systematically tests multiple orientations, ensuring robust detection regardless of document positioning.

The educational value of the project also deserves mention. Building a complete document processing system provides hands-on experience with multiple technologies including OCR, cryptographic hash chains, web services, and algorithm implementation. The project structure serves as a reference implementation for similar systems and provides a foundation for further research and development in the field.

\section{Project Objectives}

The primary objective of this project is to design and develop a web-based platform that automatically detects and redacts sensitive personally identifiable information from PDF and image documents, with specific focus on Indian identity documents including Aadhaar UIDs, PAN cards, and payment card numbers.

The project encompasses the following specific objectives:

The first objective involves building a robust PII detection system that combines multiple detection strategies to achieve high accuracy. This includes implementing the Verhoeff checksum algorithm for validating Aadhaar 12-digit numbers, the Luhn algorithm for validating payment card numbers of 13-19 digits, and structural pattern matching with entity-type validation for PAN card numbers. The detection system must operate on text extracted through OCR, converting the extracted characters into a searchable format while preserving positional information for subsequent masking operations.

The second objective requires developing a multi-orientation OCR pipeline that handles documents in various rotational states. The pipeline must test multiple preprocessing combinations including four rotation angles (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) and two image preprocessing states (original and Gaussian-blurred). The system must implement early-exit optimization to ensure reasonable processing times when documents are correctly oriented, while maintaining comprehensive scanning capability for rotated inputs.

The third objective concerns implementing configurable masking levels that cater to different compliance requirements. The system must provide three distinct masking strategies: standard masking that preserves the last four characters for reference, partial masking that preserves the first four characters, and full masking that completely obscures all detected information. Each masking strategy must be applied at the character level within detected PII regions.

The fourth objective focuses on creating a complete web application with REST API endpoints for programmatic access and an intuitive user interface for manual operations. The web interface must support drag-and-drop file upload, real-time processing status, and result visualization including detection statistics and preview of redacted documents.

The fifth objective involves implementing tamper-evident audit logging that maintains verifiable records of all processing operations. The audit system must use cryptographic hash chains to ensure that historical records cannot be modified without detection, providing compliance evidence suitable for regulatory audits and forensic analysis.

The sixth objective requires achieving acceptable processing performance with the goal of sub-300ms processing time for single-page images with correctly oriented text. The system must handle both PDF documents (with multi-page support) and common image formats (JPG, JPEG, PNG) while maintaining memory efficiency through appropriate cleanup and timeout mechanisms.

\section{Scope of Project}

The scope of this project defines the boundaries of the developed system, clarifying what the project addresses and what falls outside its intended purpose.

The project encompasses the following in-scope elements. First, the detection and redaction of three specific PII types from Indian identity documents: Aadhaar UIDs validated through Verhoeff checksum, payment card numbers validated through Luhn algorithm, and PAN card numbers validated through structural pattern matching. Second, the system processes two document categories: PDF files (with support for multiple pages converted to images) and image files in JPG, JPEG, and PNG formats. Third, the platform implements three configurable redaction levels as described previously. Fourth, the system provides a web-based user interface with drag-and-drop upload, redaction level selection, and result visualization. Fifth, the platform exposes REST API endpoints for programmatic access by other applications. Sixth, the system implements tamper-evident audit logging with SHA-256 hash chain verification. Seventh, the system maintains a zero-data-retention architecture where processed documents exist only in transient memory during the processing operation.

The project explicitly excludes certain elements from its scope. Video redaction capability remains outside the current implementation, though this is identified as a direction for future work. Multi-language OCR support focusing on English-language text extraction is the current limitation, with future plans to extend support for Devanagari, Tamil, and other Indian scripts. The system does not include integration with external storage systems or cloud platforms. Real-time document processing for streaming video content is not supported in the current version. The system does not provide batch processing capabilities for handling large volumes of documents in scheduled operations.

The assumptions underlying the project scope include that input documents will contain readable printed or typed text rather than handwritten content. The OCR accuracy depends on image quality, and the system is optimized for scanned documents or photographs of physical documents rather than screenshots of digital documents. The project assumes a standalone deployment model rather than distributed or clustered processing architecture. The implementation focuses on single-user request handling rather than concurrent bulk processing scenarios.

The constraints affecting the project include processing performance dependent on the client machine's specifications, particularly for PDF conversion and OCR operations. The NER-based detection feature requires the transformers and torch libraries, with graceful fallback to rule-based detection when these are unavailable. The system design prioritizes accuracy over speed, accepting the computational cost of multiple orientation testing to ensure comprehensive detection.
